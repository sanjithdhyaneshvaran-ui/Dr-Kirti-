Screening & early detection

Self Breast Examination

4 min read · Medically reviewed by Dr. Kirti

Every breast is different. A simple check once a month helps you learn what is normal for you, so you notice any change early. It takes about ten minutes and needs no equipment.

Why know your normal?

Breasts change with your cycle, with age and after pregnancy. Most lumps and bumps you feel are normal breast tissue.

When you check regularly, you learn how your breasts usually look and feel. That makes it much easier to notice something new.

Good to know: You are not looking for cancer. You are looking for change. Most changes turn out to be benign.

The right time to check

If you have periods

Check a few days after your period ends, when your breasts are least swollen and tender.

If you no longer have periods

Pick the same date each month, such as the first of the month, so it becomes a habit.

How to examine your breasts, step by step

Step 1 of 6

Step 1

Look in the mirror

Stand with your arms by your sides. Look at the size, shape and skin of both breasts, and at both nipples.

Tip: Some women find it easier to feel the breast in the shower, with soapy hands. Choose whichever way you will keep doing.

Changes to look and feel for

  • A new lump or thickening in the breast or armpit
  • A change in size or shape
  • Dimpling, puckering or "orange-peel" skin
  • Redness, a rash or crusting on or around the nipple
  • A nipple that has newly turned inward
  • Discharge from the nipple, especially if it is blood-stained
  • Pain in one spot that does not go away

Found something new?

Don't panic, and don't wait. Most changes are benign, such as a cyst or a fibroadenoma (a smooth, harmless lump).

Book an assessment with a breast specialist. Dr. Kirti will examine you and arrange an ultrasound or mammogram if needed, so you get a clear answer quickly.
