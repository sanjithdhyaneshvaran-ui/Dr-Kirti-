Life After Breast Cancer

3 min read · Medically reviewed by Dr. Kirti

Finishing treatment is a big milestone, but it can also bring new questions and feelings. This page covers getting back to your routine, looking after your emotional health, and what to expect from , so you can move forward with confidence.

Getting back to everyday life

There is no fixed timeline. Many women return to work and their usual activities within a few weeks to months, often gradually.

TirednessTiredness can last for months after treatment. Pace yourself and rest when you need to.
WorkConsider a phased return, with shorter days at first.
ExerciseStart gently and build up; regular activity improves energy and mood.
Intimacy and relationshipsChanges are common and can be discussed openly with Dr. Kirti.

Coping with your feelings

It is normal to feel relieved, anxious, low or uncertain once treatment ends. Some women worry about the cancer coming back, especially around check-ups.

Talking helps, whether with family, friends, other women who have been through it, or a counsellor. If low mood or worry is affecting your daily life, tell us. Support is available.

Your follow-up plan

Regular check-ups help catch any problems early and support your recovery.

Years 1 to 2A check-up every 3 to 6 months
Years 3 to 5Every 6 to 12 months
After 5 yearsOnce a year
Mammogram: usually once a year

If you are taking , keep taking it as prescribed and mention any side effects at your check-ups.

Looking after your health

  • Stay active: aim for regular walking or other exercise you enjoy
  • Eat a balanced diet with plenty of vegetables, fruit and whole grains
  • Keep to a healthy weight
  • Limit alcohol and avoid smoking

When to call us

Don't wait for your next check-up if you notice:

  • A new lump or change in either breast, the scar or the armpit
  • Bone pain that doesn't go away
  • A cough or breathlessness that doesn't settle
  • Unexplained weight loss or persistent tiredness
