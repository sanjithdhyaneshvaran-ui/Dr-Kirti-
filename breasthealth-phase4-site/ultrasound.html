Breast Ultrasound

4 min read · Medically reviewed by Dr. Kirti

A breast ultrasound uses sound waves to make a picture of the inside of the breast. It is painless, takes about 15 minutes and uses no radiation, so it is safe at any age and during pregnancy.

What is a breast ultrasound?

A small handheld probe is moved over the skin of the breast. It sends out sound waves, and the echoes create a live picture on a screen.

Ultrasound is very good at telling the difference between a fluid-filled cyst and a solid lump. It also shows the milk ducts and the lymph nodes in the armpit.

Illustration only. Only a specialist can interpret a scan.

When is a breast ultrasound advised?

Women under 40

Ultrasound is usually the first test for a younger woman with a breast concern, because younger breasts are dense and harder to see on a mammogram.

Alongside a mammogram

It is often added to a mammogram to look more closely at a lump, a duct or an area of dense tissue.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding

It is the safest breast test during pregnancy and while breastfeeding, because there is no radiation.

Using ultrasound to guide treatment and tests

Ultrasound lets the doctor see a needle in real time, so procedures are precise and quick, usually under local anaesthetic.

Needle aspiration

Draining fluid from a cyst, which often makes it disappear

Usually done under local anaesthetic

Usually done under local anaesthetic

What happens during a breast ultrasound

  1. 1 Getting ready
    You lie on your back with your arm raised above your head.
  2. 2 The scan
    A cool gel is spread on the skin, and the probe is moved gently over the breast and armpit.
  3. 3 Afterwards
    The gel is wiped off and you can go back to your day straight away.

No special preparation is needed. Wear a two-piece outfit, and bring any earlier scans or reports.
