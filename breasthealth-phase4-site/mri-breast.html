Breast MRI

4 min read · Medically reviewed by Dr. Kirti

A breast MRI uses a strong magnet and radio waves, not X-rays, to make very detailed pictures of the breast. It is not a routine test. Dr. Kirti advises it for women at higher risk, or when a mammogram or ultrasound does not give a clear answer.

What is a breast MRI?

MRI stands for magnetic resonance imaging. It shows the breast in fine detail and can find small changes that a mammogram or ultrasound may miss.

Because it is so sensitive, it can also pick up harmless changes. That is why a breast MRI should only be done after consulting your specialist, who will decide whether you need it.

When is a breast MRI advised?

01

Screening younger women at high risk

For women with a strong family history or a gene change such as BRCA1 or BRCA2, MRI may be used for screening, often alongside a mammogram and sometimes from a younger age.

02

When other tests are unclear

In some breast conditions, or in very dense breasts, a mammogram may not give a clear picture. An MRI can help.

03

Planning treatment

After a breast cancer diagnosis, MRI can show the exact size of the cancer and check the rest of both breasts, to help plan surgery.

Preparing for a breast MRI

None of these automatically stop you having an MRI. They just help the team keep you safe.

Tell the team before the scan if you:

  • Metal in your body Have a pacemaker, metal implant, clips or any metal in your body
  • Kidneys or dye allergy Have kidney problems, or an allergy to contrast dye
  • Pregnancy Are pregnant or may be pregnant
  • Breastfeeding Are breastfeeding
  • Closed spaces Feel anxious in small or closed spaces

Good to know: If you still have periods, the scan is best done in the second week of your cycle (days 7 to 14), when the breasts give the clearest pictures.

What happens during a breast MRI

  3. Change & prepare 10 min You change into a gown and remove all metal, such as jewellery and hairpins.
  4. Contrast dye 2 min A small needle is placed in your arm for a contrast dye that makes the pictures clearer.
  5. Scan 30–45 min You lie face down on a padded table, with your breasts resting in cushioned openings. The table slides into the scanner, which makes loud knocking sounds. You are given earplugs or headphones.
  6. Home No recovery time You can eat, drive and return to normal activities straight after.

What it sounds like
The scanner makes loud knocking sounds. This is normal. You'll have earplugs or headphones, and you can talk to the team at any time.
