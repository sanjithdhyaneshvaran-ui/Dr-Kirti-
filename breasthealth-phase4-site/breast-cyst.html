Benign breast conditions

Breast Cyst

5 min read · Medically reviewed by Dr. Kirti

A breast cyst is a fluid-filled sac in the breast. Cysts are very common and almost always benign. Most need no treatment — and one that is painful or large can be drained with a needle, in a short outpatient procedure, by a breast specialist in Chennai.

What is a breast cyst?

A cyst forms when fluid collects in one of the breast's milk glands. Cysts can be as small as a pea or larger than a grape, and you may have one or several, in one or both breasts.

Cysts are linked to changing hormone levels. That is why they are most common in the years before menopause, and often settle afterwards — unless you take hormone replacement therapy (HRT), which can cause new cysts to form.

Good to know: a simple cyst is not cancer and does not increase your risk of breast cancer. Any change within a cyst, though, should be assessed by a specialist.

Signs and symptoms

  • A smooth, round or oval lump that moves under your fingers
  • It may feel soft, or quite firm
  • Tenderness or pain, especially before a period
  • A lump that changes in size with your cycle
  • Sometimes no symptoms at all — found on a scan

Because a cyst can feel like any other lump, every new breast lump should be checked. An examination and ultrasound can confirm a cyst quickly.

How a breast cyst is diagnosed

  1. 1 Clinical examination
    A careful examination of both breasts and the armpits.
  2. 2 Breast ultrasound
    The best test to confirm a cyst and see whether it is simple or complex. About breast ultrasound
  3. 3 Mammogram, if needed
    Depending on your age. About mammograms

Ultrasound-guided aspiration, step by step

If a cyst is painful, large or needs a closer look, it can be drained with a needle. Ultrasound guidance means the needle goes exactly where it is needed.

  1. 01

    Find the cyst

    An ultrasound shows exactly where the cyst is.

  2. 02

    Prepare

    The skin is cleaned and, if needed, numbed with a local anaesthetic.

  3. 03

    Drain

    A needle draws out the fluid, and the cyst usually disappears.

  4. 04

    Check & follow up

    Blood-stained fluid is sent for testing, and a follow-up check is arranged if needed.

A short outpatient procedure. Most women go home straight afterwards and carry on with their day.

Breast cysts: frequently asked questions

Simple cysts do not turn into cancer and do not raise your risk. Complex cysts are checked more closely, because some need a biopsy to be sure.

Most women find it only mildly uncomfortable. It takes a few minutes, and a painful cyst usually feels better straight away once the fluid is removed.

Sometimes. Cysts can refill, or new ones can form, especially before menopause. A cyst that comes back can usually be drained again.

Very rarely. Surgery is considered only if a cyst keeps coming back, contains blood-stained fluid, or has solid areas that need to be examined.

Cysts are linked to changing hormone levels, which shift most in the years before menopause. They usually become less common afterwards, unless you take hormone replacement therapy.
