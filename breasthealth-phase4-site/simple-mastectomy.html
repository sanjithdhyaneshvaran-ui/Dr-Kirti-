Treatment

Mastectomy

4 min read · Medically reviewed by Dr. Kirti

A removes the whole breast. It may be advised when the cancer is large or in more than one area, or chosen to lower the risk of cancer in the future. Today there are several types, and many women can have the breast rebuilt at the same operation.

When is a mastectomy advised?

Usually advised when…

  • The cancer is large compared with the size of the breast
  • There are cancers in several parts of the breast
  • Radiotherapy is not possible after breast conservation
  • The cancer has come back after earlier breast conservation
  • You carry a gene change such as BRCA and choose surgery to

Your choice matters too.

Some women who could keep their breast prefer a mastectomy. Dr. Kirti will help you weigh up the options.

Three types of mastectomy

Simple mastectomy

The whole breast is removed, including the nipple, and overlying skin. It leaves a straight scar across the chest, and that side of the chest is flat. Many women wear an external breast afterwards. Reconstruction can still be done later.

Kept
  • Chest muscle
Removed
  • Breast tissue
  • Nipple and areola
  • Overlying skin
ReconstructionCan still be done later

Skin-sparing mastectomy

The breast tissue, nipple and areola are removed, but most of the breast skin is kept. This is usually done when immediate reconstruction is planned, as the skin forms a natural envelope for the new breast, with minimal scarring.

Kept
  • Most of the breast skin
Removed
  • Breast tissue
  • Nipple and areola
ReconstructionUsually immediate

Nipple-sparing mastectomy

Only the breast tissue is removed, keeping the nipple, areola and skin. It is usually followed by reconstruction with an implant or your own tissue. It is suitable when the cancer is well away from the nipple, and the scar is often hidden below the breast.

Kept
  • Skin
  • Nipple and areola
Removed
  • Breast tissue
ReconstructionUsually follows, with an implant or your own tissue

Recovering from a mastectomy

  3. Day of surgery Usually one or two nights in hospital; longer with reconstruction.
  4. First week A drain may stay in for a few days; gentle arm exercises begin.
  5. 2–3 weeks Light daily activities; avoid heavy lifting.
  6. 4–6 weeks Most women return to normal activities and driving.

A physiotherapist will show you arm and shoulder exercises to help you recover full movement.

Life after a mastectomy

Breast prosthesis

A soft, weighted breast form that fits inside your bra. A temporary soft one is used while you heal, and a permanent one can be fitted after about six weeks.

About breast prosthesis →

Emotional recovery

Losing a breast can affect how you feel about your body. These feelings are normal, and support is available. Talk to Dr. Kirti and the team at any time.
