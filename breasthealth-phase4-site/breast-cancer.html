Understanding breast cancer

What is breast cancer?

Breast cancer starts when cells in the breast begin to grow out of control. Most breast cancers begin in the milk ducts or in the .

Found early, while it is small and has not spread, breast cancer can very often be treated successfully, and many women are able to keep their breast.

Most breast lumps are not cancer. But every new change deserves a specialist's check, so you get a clear answer.

Signs to know

When to see a breast specialist

See a specialist if you notice any of these changes. Most turn out to be benign, but it is always worth checking.

A new lumpIn the breast or the armpit, even if it is painless.
A change in size or shapeOne breast looks or feels different from usual.
Dimpling of the skinPuckering, or skin that looks like orange peel.
A nipple turning inwardsA nipple that newly pulls in or changes position.
Nipple dischargeEspecially if it is bloody or comes from one side only.
Skin changesRedness, scaling, a rash or thickening of the skin.

Early breast cancer often causes no symptoms at all. That is why regular checks and screening matter.
