Oncoplastic Breast Surgery & Reconstruction

5 min read · Medically reviewed by Dr. Kirti

surgery combines breast cancer surgery with plastic surgery techniques. The aim is simple: remove the cancer completely, and leave you with a breast that looks and feels as natural as possible. When the whole breast needs to be removed, reconstruction can rebuild it, at the same time or later.

Treating the cancer, caring for your shape

In standard surgery, removing part of the breast can leave a dent or change its shape. Oncoplastic surgery prevents this by reshaping or replacing the tissue during the same operation.

It means more women can keep their breast, with a better-looking result, and without compromising the cancer treatment.

Partial reconstruction: keeping your breast

Moving tissue (volume displacement)

The remaining breast tissue is rearranged to fill the space left by the cancer. When combined with a breast reduction or lift, this is called , and it suits women with larger breasts.

Replacing tissue (volume replacement)

Tissue from nearby, such as the side of the chest, is moved in to fill the space. This suits women with smaller breasts, where there is less tissue to rearrange.

Finishing touches

Can be done later

Symmetrising procedures

After surgery on one breast, the other may be larger or lower. A reduction, lift or enlargement of the other breast helps both match.

Can be done later

(fat grafting)

Fat is gently taken from the tummy or thighs and injected into the breast to restore lost volume, smooth dents or improve the skin. It uses your own tissue and leaves almost invisible scars.

Can be done later

Nipple reconstruction

A new nipple can be made from the skin of the breast, and the areola added with medical tattooing. It does not have the same feeling as a natural nipple, but it completes the look of the breast.

  3. Day of surgery One to five nights in hospital, depending on the operation.
