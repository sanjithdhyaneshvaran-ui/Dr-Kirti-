Understanding your diagnosis

Types of Breast Cancer

4 min read · Medically reviewed by Dr. Kirti

Breast cancer is not one single disease. Its type depends on where in the breast it starts and whether it has spread beyond where it began. Knowing the type helps Dr. Kirti plan the treatment that is right for you.

Where breast cancer begins

The breast is made of , which make milk, and , which carry milk to the nipple. Most breast cancers start in the cells lining the ducts or the lobules.

Cancer is described as non-invasive if it stays inside the duct, and if it has grown through the duct wall into the surrounding breast tissue.

Simplified illustration.

The three main types

Ductal carcinoma (DCIS)

Abnormal cells are found inside the milk ducts but have not spread into the surrounding tissue. It is non-invasive and is often called stage 0. DCIS is usually found on a mammogram before there are any symptoms, and it is very treatable.

How commonLess common

Invasive breast cancer

Cancer cells have grown beyond the duct or lobule into nearby breast tissue, and may spread to the in the armpit.

  • Invasive ductal carcinoma, also called "no special type", is the most common type of breast cancer.
  • Invasive lobular carcinoma starts in the lobules. It can be harder to see on a mammogram, so an MRI is sometimes added.
How commonMost common

Metastatic breast cancer

Breast cancer that has spread to other parts of the body, such as the bones, liver, lungs or brain. It is also called stage 4 or secondary breast cancer. It is usually not curable, but it can often be controlled with treatment for many years.

How commonRare at first diagnosis

Less common types

A rare, fast-growing type that makes the breast red, swollen and warm, often without a lump. It needs urgent assessment.

What the grade means

The pathologist also gives the cancer a grade from 1 to 3, based on how different the cells look from normal cells.

Grade 1 Cells look closest to normal and tend to grow slowly
Grade 2 In between
Grade 3 Cells look very different and tend to grow faster

Grade is different from stage. The grade describes the cells; the stage describes size and spread.
