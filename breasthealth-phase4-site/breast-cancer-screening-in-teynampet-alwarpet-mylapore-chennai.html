Screening & Early Detection

5 min read · Medically reviewed by Dr. Kirti

Breast cancer found early is easier to treat, and most women go on to live full lives.

Understanding screening

Why screen when you feel well?

Screening means checking the breasts before there are any symptoms. It can find changes that are too small to feel, including non-cancerous, pre-cancerous and early cancerous changes.

Finding a problem early usually means simpler treatment, a wider choice of surgery, and a better outcome. That is why screening is for women who feel completely well.

Screening is for women without symptoms. If you have already noticed a lump, pain or any other change, don't wait for a routine screen — book an assessment.

Ways to screen the breast

01 Self Breast Examination A simple monthly check at home, so you know what is normal for you. Learn how to check → At home 02 Mammogram A low-dose X-ray of the breast — the main screening test for women over 40. About mammograms → From 40 03 Breast Ultrasound A painless, radiation-free scan, often used for younger women and alongside a mammogram. About breast ultrasound → No radiation 04 Breast MRI A detailed scan for women at higher risk, or when other tests are not clear. About breast MRI → Specialist advised

Dr. Kirti also offers a clinical breast examination — a careful check of both breasts and underarms by a specialist — at every screening consultation.

Your screening plan

Which screening is right for you?

Screening depends on your age and your risk. Dr. Kirti will tailor a plan for you, but this is the usual guide.

How often Every 2 years

Women 40 and over

  • Monthly self breast examination
  • A mammogram every two years
  • Ultrasound alongside the mammogram when needed

You may be at higher risk if a close relative had breast or ovarian cancer, if you carry a gene change such as BRCA1 or BRCA2, or if you had radiotherapy to the chest at a young age.

Don't wait for your next screen if you notice…

Most changes turn out to be benign, but they should be checked promptly.

Book a Prompt Assessment
  • A new lump or thickening in the breast or underarm
  • A change in the size or shape of a breast
  • Skin dimpling, puckering or an "orange-peel" texture
  • A nipple that has newly turned inward
  • Blood-stained discharge from the nipple
  • A rash or crusting around the nipple that does not heal

Breast screening: FAQs

Monthly self-checks can start in your twenties. Most women are advised to start mammograms at 40. If you are at higher risk, Dr. Kirti may advise starting earlier.
